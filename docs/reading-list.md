# Reading list

Sources for mPod's page copy, gathered 2026-11-05; every link is a Wayback Machine snapshot, checked to load; summaries are our own words.

## The release, 2001

- [Apple Presents iPod](https://web.archive.org/web/20011109115712/http://www.apple.com:80/pr/library/2001/oct/23ipod.html). Apple press release, Oct. 23, 2001. The launch text: a 6.5 oz player, 5 GB drive, Auto-Sync with iTunes, FireWire transfer and 10 hours of battery.
- [Apple's 'Breakthrough' iPod](https://web.archive.org/web/20011118135607/http://www.wired.com:80/news/technology/0,1282,47805,00.html). Wired News, Brad King and Farhad Manjoo, Oct. 23, 2001. Launch-day report from Cupertino, setting Jobs's pitch against disappointed Mac users and cheaper rivals with more storage.
- [Pundits compliment, criticize iPod](https://web.archive.org/web/20011116111643/http://maccentral.macworld.com:80/news/0110/24.ipodreact.php). MacCentral (Macworld), Peter Cohen, Oct. 24, 2001. A day-after press round-up: critics hit the price and the Mac-only limit, others praised the design and ease of use.
- [NY Times impressed with iPod's FireWire capabilities](https://web.archive.org/web/20011116104533/http://maccentral.macworld.com:80/news/0110/26.nytimesipod.php). MacCentral, Oct. 26, 2001. Covers David Pogue's New York Times column, which doubted the breakthrough claim but found it beautiful and the price steep.
- [Apple releases iPod](https://web.archive.org/web/20020826011100/http://slashdot.org:80/articles/01/10/23/1816257.shtml). Slashdot, posted by CmdrTaco, Oct. 23, 2001. A famously dismissive one-line post, which drew about 1,075 comments.
- [Apple's New Thing](https://web.archive.org/web/20051126085341/http://forums.macrumors.com:80/showthread.php?t=500). MacRumors forums, Oct. 23, 2001. A six-page live reaction thread of first-day doubt, with jokes about the name and complaints about the price.

## Minimalism, Braun to iPod

- [Good design](https://web.archive.org/web/20120803192710/https://www.vitsoe.com/gb/about/good-design). Vitsœ, page captured 2012; Rams wrote the principles in the late 1970s and 1980s. Rams's ten principles, each paired with a Braun object, opening with his worry about confused forms, colours and noises.
- [1960s Braun Products Hold the Secrets to Apple's Future](https://web.archive.org/web/20080116080838/http://gizmodo.com:80/343641/1960s-braun-products-hold-the-secrets-to-apples-future). Gizmodo, Jesús Díaz, Jan. 14, 2008. The side-by-side photo essay that made the T3 and iPod comparison famous, treating Apple's work as evolution rather than theft.
- [T3 Transistor Radio](https://web.archive.org/web/20110703185411/http://research.microsoft.com/en-us/um/people/bibuxton/buxtoncollection/detail.aspx?id=179). Microsoft Research, Bill Buxton's notes, captured 2011. A design researcher's short note crediting the 1958 T3 as the model for the first four iPod generations.
- [He articulated the way it should be](https://web.archive.org/web/20241104122048/https://rams-foundation.org/magazine/interviews/jonathan-ive/). rams foundation, interview with Jonathan Ive by Gerrit Terstiege, undated; first archived Nov. 4, 2024. Ive describes his debt to Rams and why the iPod was white, and denies copying the T3 outright.
- [The Guts of a New Machine](https://web.archive.org/web/20090801082931/http://www.nytimes.com:80/2003/11/30/magazine/30IPOD.html). New York Times Magazine, Rob Walker, Nov. 30, 2003. An essay on how the iPod became an icon, layer by layer, with interviews with Jobs and Ive. Paywalled.

## One device, one job: now

- [Why people are buying iPods again](https://web.archive.org/web/20260221190536/https://www.axios.com/2026/02/21/ipods-music-genz-nostalgia). Axios, Sami Sparber, Feb. 21, 2026. Ties the iPod comeback to digital burnout, with rising eBay searches and Cal Newport on single-purpose technology.
- [Bring on defunct: The iPod enthralls young music listeners](https://web.archive.org/web/20260304044344/https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/mar/02/bring-on-defunct-the-ipod-enthralls-young-music-li/). The New York Times, this copy in The Spokesman-Review, Kalley Huang, about 1 or 2 Mar 2026. Young listeners turn to old iPods as songs vanish from streaming, prices rise and notifications pull them away. Metered.
- [25 years later, is it time for a new iPod?](https://web.archive.org/web/20260423154341/https://www.lowpass.cc/p/ipod-anniversary-mp3-dap-player-revival-sleevenote). Lowpass, Janko Roettgers, about April 23, 2026. Searches for MP3 player have tripled since autumn, and a small team is building a new one. Paywalled.
- [On Digital Minimalism](https://web.archive.org/web/20161219201208/http://calnewport.com/blog/2016/12/18/on-digital-minimalism/). Study Hacks, Cal Newport's blog, Dec. 18, 2016. The first statement of the idea: question which digital tools add value, then clear the rest away.
- [Can the Light Phone help us switch off?](https://web.archive.org/web/20240827135102/https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/views/people/67647/can-the-light-phone-help-us-switch-off). Prospect, Ella Glover, Aug. 27, 2024. Interview with co-founder Joe Hollier: no apps, no social media, no sticky design, just a handful of tools. Metered.
- [Dumbphones a smart move? Some young people seeking free time and friends say yes.](https://web.archive.org/web/20240923201933/https://www.csmonitor.com/USA/Society/2024/0923/Dumbphones-a-smart-move-Some-young-people-seeking-free-time-and-friends-say-yes). Christian Science Monitor, Danish Bajwa, Sept. 23, 2024. Harvard and MIT students on the Light Phone III; one cut screen time from 11 hours to three or four. Metered.

Not found: The Guardian (nothing archived at all), the URL of Pogue's 2001 column, any BBC or CNN iPod story from 2001, and the Dezeen 2011 piece, which survives only as a stub.
